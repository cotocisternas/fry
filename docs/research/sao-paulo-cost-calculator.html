What the fixed infrastructure example includes
  • Six m7i.2xlarge Linux workers across three zones: 48 vCPU and 192 GiB total before a failure; six 100 GiB gp3 disks.
  • One standard-support EKS cluster; RDS PostgreSQL db.r7g.2xlarge Multi-AZ with one standby and 3 TiB gp3.
  • Three NAT gateways’ hourly fees and six public IPv4 addresses. One NLB’s hourly fee is included in its separate line above.
  • 730 compute hours; on-demand pricing. These services, sizes, and three-zone layout are costing examples, not selected architecture.
Costs still to add

Variable NAT processing and applicable cross-zone traffic; outbound email and relay fees; extra reads/retries, old mailbox history, logging/audit, backup excess, support, taxes, staging, and migration overlap. External IMAP collection may add substantial NAT traffic. At São Paulo’s rate, 10 TB through NAT adds approximately $930 before other transfer charges.

Units and model limits

Message KB and displayed TB are decimal. S3 storage is converted to binary billed GB. Network charges conservatively use decimal GB; reconcile actual metering in a deployment test. The shared 100 GB egress allowance is assumed available. NLB counts one incoming copy and one fetched copy through TCP listeners; connection-based charges may exceed the byte calculation. The example assumes direct SMTP ingress and S3 gateway-endpoint access, so variable collection/outbound NAT traffic is additional. S3 Standard’s built-in multi-AZ replication is already included in its price.

# FRY discovery

FRY — The delivery boy — is being explored as an email gateway and router for business document exchange. This document tracks the interview; proposals are not accepted architecture decisions.

## Established from the initial brief

- Business exchanges involve XML, PDF, and other documents carried in email.
- Existing integrations send and receive through SMTP and IMAP.
- Many mailboxes and their storage create operational problems; follow-up identified high volume and throughput across multiple servers using NFS as the central concern.
- FRY should organize inboxes across multiple custom domains and filter incoming garbage so useful messages and documents reach consumers.
- Go is the requested implementation language.
- Distributed persistence, including databases or object storage, is under consideration.
- Proton's Go libraries are a candidate to investigate, not a selected foundation.

## Current workload and operational problem

The user reports running multiple mail servers to handle load, using NFS with a conventional Dovecot/Postfix setup, and finding that arrangement operationally painful. High volume and high throughput are the primary drivers for FRY. The user attributes the shared-storage workaround to the existing stack's lack of built-in distributed-system mechanisms; this is their operational assessment, not a verified claim about every edition or deployment of those products.

The specific failure mode and bottleneck have not been measured here. The exact mailbox storage format remains unknown; “mboxes” must not be interpreted as confirmation of the mbox file format. No storage backend or distributed coordination model has been selected. Candidate architectures will need evidence for aggregate throughput, burst handling, and behavior under node/storage failures.

### Reported scale

- Aggregate incoming volume is estimated at tens of millions of emails per day; no exact daily design target has been agreed.
- Traffic sometimes arrives steadily, but bulk arrivals are more common. A large business may send approximately 500,000 emails in one batch.
- A single inbox can receive most or all of a roughly 500,000-email batch. Single-inbox ingest, processing, mailbox-view growth, and access therefore need explicit capacity evaluation alongside aggregate throughput.
- The user explicitly does not know the batch arrival duration. Defer it to measurement; do not infer a peak rate from the batch count or daily volume. Overlap between senders and the overall traffic distribution across organizations/inboxes are also unknown. A sender starting a batch does not establish how quickly FRY receives it.
- A typical complete incoming email, including attachments, is usually under 1 MB. This is a rough size category, not a measured mean or an enforced maximum. Asked about the largest complete email including encoded attachments, the user answered “unknown, 200mb maybe?” Record approximately 200 MB as a tentative large-message test case, not a measured maximum or agreed admission limit. The size distribution, frequency of such large messages, and required upper limit remain unknown.
- For sizing illustration only: 10 million messages/day averages about 116 messages/second. A batch of 500,000 arriving over five minutes adds about 1,667 messages/second during that window; five minutes is hypothetical, not a reported measurement or target.

The user accepts a temporary processing backlog during bursts: messages can be durably received and take several minutes to finish checks and become available to consumers. The maximum acceptable delay and backlog size remain open. A later measurement exercise should establish arrival rates over short windows and byte throughput; no access to production telemetry has been requested or assumed.

## Delivery milestones

The first production milestone proves conventional email operation: SMTP reception and outbound relay handoff, external IMAP collection, conventional shared IMAP mailboxes, isolated organizations/custom domains, distributed storage, the agreed mailbox/recovery lifecycle, and the guarantees of the selected deployment profile. See [conventional email first](../adr/0014-prove-conventional-email-first.md).

Optional filtering, routing, and application delivery features are deferred to later milestones. Their decisions below remain the intended behavior when implemented; they are not required for the first production milestone. Application-facing API/webhook delivery, independent application views, document/ZIP inspection, and filtering-related review workflows belong to that deferred scope. The first milestone requires a web administration interface alongside an administrative API and CLI for organizations, domains, mailboxes, and credentials; both central FRY operators and organization managers restricted to their authorized organization scope are required. Administrator sign-in must support both FRY-managed accounts and SSO in the first milestone. SSO means generic OpenID Connect, available in this milestone although initial operation uses internal FRY accounts. No specific provider or SAML requirement is selected. One shared OIDC provider per independent FRY installation is sufficient initially. First OIDC login automatically provisions accounts and access from configured group/claim mappings. Exact organization/role mapping rules, subsequent access changes, detailed permissions, and workflows remain open. These are distinct from the deferred application-delivery API. The first rollout must import existing mailbox history and state, including folders, message dates, and read/unread flags, for cutover. A planned final cutover pause is allowed after background copying, with affected clients paused and arrivals queued or deferred during final synchronization and switching. The planned pause is limited to 30 minutes for affected mailboxes, excluding background copying: mail reception and mailbox access must resume within that window, while client resynchronization may continue afterward. This is distinct from failure-recovery targets and still needs validation at representative scale. Migration mechanics, concrete client compatibility checks, workload measurements, and validated recovery behavior remain open.

## Agreed decisions

### Compatibility baseline and optional features

The user clarified that attachments can be anything, and filtering and routing must be optional rather than prerequisites for FRY to work. Most existing integrations expect a traditional email system designed for human mailbox users; development teams need time to adopt the additional integration model. FRY must therefore receive, store, and expose mail without requiring document classification, routing rules, or fallback-application setup. Accepting arbitrary types means preserving their bytes, not promising inspection support for all formats. Business document exchange remains the product purpose.

The checking, quarantine, ZIP inspection, and rule-based delivery decisions below apply when the corresponding optional features are enabled. A deliberately disabled check does not block delivery; an enabled required check that cannot run still holds mail under the previously agreed policy. Protocol, access-control, isolation, durability, and resource-limit requirements remain separate. Conventional shared IMAP mailbox state is the default: clients using the same mailbox share reads, flags, moves, and deletions. Independent per-application views are optional. All optional filtering starts disabled for newly configured inboxes, including spam and malware checks; administrators enable what they need. Document-format filtering and application routing are opt-in. Configuration scope, administrator permissions, policy changes for already accepted mail, and migration behavior remain open. See [optional filtering and routing](../adr/0013-optional-filtering-and-routing.md). This clarification supersedes earlier unconditional filtering/routing requirements.


- FRY focuses on automated business document exchange, with human review of exceptions. See [product scope](../adr/0001-business-document-gateway.md).
- FRY supports both direct SMTP reception and collection from existing IMAP mailboxes. Ongoing IMAP collection supports configurable source folders in the first milestone; it is not limited to INBOX and is distinct from one-time history migration. Each selected source folder maps to a configurable destination folder in a FRY inbox. After collection, FRY keeps an independent copy: later source-side reads/flags, moves, and deletions do not modify or remove it. Optional source cleanup and final synchronization during one-time migration remain separate. Mapping configuration changes, destination-folder creation/rename/deletion behavior, source-folder selection/discovery, initial-copy metadata, and repeat-collection detection remain open; this decision does not establish deduplication when source moves expose new folder/message identities. External source cleanup is configurable per connection, with source copies kept by default; removal can be enabled only after durable collection across FRY's independent sites. See [ingress modes](../adr/0002-direct-and-imap-ingress.md). The first rollout must import existing mailbox history and state for cutover, including folders, message dates, and read/unread flags. Migration is distinct from ongoing collection; a planned final synchronization/switching pause is allowed after background copying, with affected clients paused and arrivals queued or deferred. The pause may last at most 30 minutes, excluding background copying; mail reception and mailbox access must resume by then, while clients may continue resynchronizing afterward. Coordinated client checkpoint reset and resynchronization are acceptable if FRY assigns new IMAP identifiers; source identifiers need not be preserved. This does not waive stable identifiers during normal FRY operation or failover. Transfer mechanism, final synchronization mechanics, reset procedure, handling of old mail fetched again, verification, rollback, collection/checkpoint behavior, and cleanup timing/mechanics remain open. Source-history deletion and server decommissioning have not been authorized.
- FRY preserves IMAP access for existing consumers and offers API/webhooks. Existing integrations use the richer category of IMAP operations, including folders, moving messages, or server-side searches. Required searches cover email headers, dates, and flags such as read/unread; body text and attachment-content search are not business requirements. Exact folder/move operations still need clarification. See [consumer interfaces](../adr/0003-imap-and-api-consumers.md). API/webhook contracts remain open; Gluon is still a candidate, not a selected library.
- The first production milestone requires web administration alongside an administrative API and CLI for managing organizations, domains, mailboxes, and credentials. Both global/central FRY operators and organization managers/administrators are required. Organization managers are internal Customer Satisfaction and Professional Services staff from the user's company; global operators are its Technology and Development teams. Organization managers administer domains, mailboxes, and credentials within their authorized organization scope; they may manage only explicitly assigned organizations, potentially several per person, with no blanket access from team membership. Only global operators and their configured OIDC mappings may grant or revoke manager assignments; managers cannot delegate administrative access. Organization managers may open or download complete emails within that scope, and central operators may do so across organizations within the deployment, through explicit content-access permissions for troubleshooting and recovery. Record content access in an audit log. Configuration authority alone does not grant this permission. Administrator sign-in must support both FRY-managed accounts and SSO initially. Generic OpenID Connect must be available initially, with internal FRY accounts used for initial operation. No specific provider or SAML requirement is selected. One shared OIDC provider per independent FRY installation is sufficient; per-organization providers are not required initially. First OIDC login automatically provisions accounts and access from configured identity-provider groups/claims, without requiring invitations or pre-created accounts. Explicit group/claim mappings may grant global-operator access too. Exact mappings and assignment workflows, reconciliation between local grants and OIDC mappings, unmatched-identity handling, identity linking, subsequent access changes, remaining permissions, impersonation privileges, audit fields/retention/access, and workflows remain open. This does not bring the deferred application-facing delivery API into the first milestone.
- Existing applications can switch to new FRY-issued usernames/passwords for IMAP and SMTP submission at first rollout. Existing identity-system integration and preservation of old credentials are not prerequisites. Credential scope, provisioning, rotation/revocation, and application authentication mechanisms remain open. Administrator sign-in separately requires both FRY-managed accounts and SSO in the first milestone.
- FRY supports isolated organizations, each with its own domains, inboxes, and access permissions, even if only one organization uses it initially. See [organization isolation](../adr/0004-organization-isolation.md). The mechanism providing isolation remains open.
- Each incoming custom domain belongs exclusively to one organization within a FRY installation; addresses under that domain cannot be split across organizations. This does not change approved outgoing send-on-behalf-of identities or impose cross-installation uniqueness. For direct SMTP reception, unconfigured recipients are rejected by default; an explicitly configured catch-all inbox per domain may receive them instead. The catch-all belongs to the domain's organization, applies to otherwise unconfigured addresses, and is separate from the deferred application-routing fallback. Domain onboarding verification and ownership transfer remain open.
- An inbox is an organization-owned destination that can receive mail through multiple email addresses, including addresses across that organization's domains. By default, IMAP clients using the same mailbox share reads, flags, folder moves, and deletions. Optional independent per-application mailbox views isolate those changes between applications. The first milestone also supports one configured incoming address delivering to several distinct inboxes within the same organization, as a distribution alias. This is conventional address resolution, separate from deferred application routing; each destination inbox has its own mailbox state. Within one SMTP submission, each distinct destination inbox receives one copy, preserving the relevant recipient-address metadata even when several addresses or alias expansions reach it. This does not deduplicate separate submissions or messages by Message-ID. Metadata exposure, alias configuration limits, and the exact folder layout remain open. This does not establish cross-organization distribution aliases or external forwarding.
- A message can be delivered to multiple applications with independent delivery progress; completion or failure for one application does not prevent delivery to another. For API/webhooks, delivery completes on a separate application acknowledgment after it downloads and safely stores the email; timed-out deliveries reuse the same delivery ID, and consumers must deduplicate by that ID. For optional independent IMAP deliveries, delivery completes when the message is safely available in the application's own mailbox view, without waiting for reads, moves, deletion, or another application acknowledgment. FRY does not wait for business processing to finish. See [independent application deliveries](../adr/0011-independent-application-deliveries.md). Exact acknowledgment API details and retry limits/scheduling remain open.
- When application routing is enabled, FRY selects receiving applications using all matching routing rules based on sender, recipient, headers, and attachment type. Each distinct matching application receives one logical delivery for that message, even when several rules select it; this does not prevent retry attempts under the same delivery ID. Each inbox using application routing requires a fallback application for messages that pass FRY's checks but match no route. Rule changes during processing/replay and precise matching operators remain open. Business-content validation remains downstream.
- Each selected application receives the complete email with all attachments available, even when a rule matched only one attachment type. See [whole-message routing](../adr/0012-deliver-complete-emails.md). Webhook applications receive a small notification carrying IDs and fetch the complete email through an authenticated API. Webhook success confirms notification receipt only; delivery stays pending until the separate acknowledgment after download and safe storage.
- When ZIP inspection is enabled, FRY inspects documents inside ZIP attachments to identify document types and evaluate routing rules, with extraction limits. This inspection preserves the complete-email delivery model, including the original ZIP attachment. In the first release, password-protected ZIPs quarantine the whole email as uninspectable; configured-password decryption is outside that release's scope. Designated organization security reviewers (for their own organization's mail) and central FRY operators may override this quarantine with a recorded reason; the original uninspectable finding is preserved. Corrupt archives, limit-exceeded behavior, supported nesting, exact limits, and access to extracted members remain open.
- Temporary processing backlogs during bursts are acceptable after safe receipt; checking and consumer availability may follow several minutes later. See [buffered processing](../adr/0005-buffer-processing-during-bursts.md). No exact latency limit, backlog limit, or queue technology has been selected.
- FRY prioritizes fair service across organizations during bursts: a large batch may take longer to drain so other organizations' processing latency is protected. The scheduling mechanism, resource budgets, and measurable latency/fairness targets remain open.
- Processing and delivery within an inbox may occur out of arrival order, allowing parallel work, while preserving receipt timestamps. FRY does not promise arrival-ordered delivery. This does not waive IMAP protocol/session correctness or stable delivery identities.
- Messages suspected of spam with uncertain classification go to quarantine, outside normal IMAP/API/webhook delivery. Authorized reviewers can inspect and release them. Unreleased quarantined emails follow the organization's normal retention period plus grace, then expire with alerts and a recorded quarantine-expiry outcome. Unsupported-document handling remains open.
- If a scanner flags an email or attachment as malware, the whole email is quarantined. Both explicitly designated organization security reviewers (for their own organization's mail) and central FRY operators may override the finding with a recorded reason; a clean rescan is not mandatory for this override. The override is a recorded release decision and does not erase the scanner's finding or bypass other required checks that remain unavailable. Central operators also have separately granted, audited content-access permissions for troubleshooting and recovery; other privileged operations remain undecided.
- If a required spam or malware check cannot run because its service is unavailable, FRY holds already accepted mail pending and retries. Required checks must produce results before delivery; adverse results follow the agreed quarantine/review policy. An unavailable check is not a spam/malware verdict and is not bypassed by the malware override privilege. If checks remain unavailable beyond retention plus the configurable grace period, FRY records a processing failure and expires the email with alerts, without delivering it unchecked. Overload behavior before acceptance remains open.
- When filtering is enabled, FRY checks email/security and attachment formats; receiving applications validate document schemas and business content. See [validation boundary](../adr/0006-business-validation-belongs-to-consumers.md). The exact format checks and supported document types remain open.
- The first release accepts outgoing email from business applications and sends it through an existing SMTP relay. See [outbound relay](../adr/0007-outbound-through-existing-relay.md). Each installation has a shared default SMTP relay; an organization may opt out and configure its own last-mile MTA server and credentials. FRY still hands mail to the selected downstream MTA. If the selected MTA is unavailable, FRY durably accepts outgoing submissions, queues them, and owns retries to that destination. Message bytes and pending queue state must be recoverable under the active deployment profile and agreed degraded-operation policy. FRY acceptance, downstream MTA acceptance, and final recipient delivery are distinct outcomes. Retries have a configurable bounded period; if handoff is still unsuccessful at expiry, FRY stops retrying, sends a delivery-failure notice to the submitting sender, and records the failure for administrators. Terminal retry failure does not itself imply immediate removal of original bytes. The user explicitly leaves the default retry duration undecided; defer it to evaluation rather than inventing a value. Submission details, configuration permissions, queue admission/capacity limits, retry duration/default/scope and clock start, retry scheduling, handling permanent rejection before the deadline, failure-notice addressing/delivery, post-failure retention, ambiguous downstream acknowledgments, changes affecting queued messages, relay failover policy, and delivery-status reporting remain open. Automatic fallback from a selected organization MTA to the shared relay has not been authorized.
- Outgoing sender domains or addresses must be explicitly authorized for the submitting organization, including approved send-on-behalf-of addresses. Authentication alone does not permit arbitrary sender identities. Assigned organization managers and global operators may approve these authorizations, including send-on-behalf-of identities, within their respective organization scopes. FRY must verify control or delegation before enabling a new outgoing sender domain or address; only global operators may bypass verification through a recorded override. Organization managers cannot bypass it. Verification mechanisms, evidence lifecycle/revocation, override audit details, envelope/header field enforcement, forwarding, and null reverse-path handling remain open. Incoming-domain onboarding is a separate unresolved policy.
- Original emails and their attachments remain recoverable after consumers delete messages from inboxes, for a configurable retention period per organization starting at FRY's durable receipt. Processing delays and delivery retries do not reset that clock. The expected duration is days, possibly months; no exact default or maximum has been chosen. Outstanding application acknowledgments, unavailable required checks, and unreleased quarantine use the same bounded retention-plus-grace policy, with alerts and the appropriate delivery-failure, processing-failure, or quarantine-expiry outcome. See [original retention](../adr/0008-retain-originals-independently-of-inboxes.md). In default shared IMAP mailboxes, visible messages remain until clients delete them, even if unread and older than the recovery-retention period. Expiry applies to retained recovery copies and must preserve bytes needed by live mailbox messages; total storage is therefore not bounded by the recovery window alone. Permanent deletion does not restart recovery retention: old messages become eligible for cleanup once no live mailbox, pending delivery, or applicable retention/grace requirement needs their bytes. Once the final mailbox, pending-delivery/work, and recovery-retention/grace reference ends, physical removal from active storage must complete within 24 hours by default, with a configurable deadline. This is an eligibility-based cleanup clock, not a new recovery-retention period; backup/snapshot erasure is outside this answered scope. Grace duration/configuration scope, recovery interfaces, exact deletion/expunge semantics, and lifetime policy for optional independent application views remain open.
- FRY must support both self-hosted and managed cloud deployments, with multiple independent installations whose topology and geographic constraints can differ: single-region, multi-region, or on-premises. Data residency matters for some installations; no universal country, region, or provider has been selected. See [deployment portability](../adr/0009-self-hosted-and-managed-cloud.md). Storage/database products, deployment packaging, and supported operational configurations remain open.
- AWS is selected for the first production rollout, with Kubernetes preferred if feasible. Amazon EKS is a candidate implementation, not yet selected. The user raised whether AWS permits SMTP infrastructure; this requires distinguishing receiving mail, outbound relay submission, and direct outbound port-25 delivery. Primary-source research distinguishes the default outbound public-port-25 restriction from SMTP hosting; the user confirms that existing downstream MTAs support authenticated SMTP on 587 or 465. That reported outbound path avoids this specific restriction. Incoming SMTP through an NLB on port 25 to a private FRY target is a candidate requiring validation. See [AWS SMTP feasibility](../research/aws-smtp-feasibility.md). The first rollout will use one AWS region across multiple availability zones. Whole-region-loss recovery belongs to a later profile. The first profile must survive loss of one entire availability zone without losing accepted email and restore mail reception and mailbox access within five minutes. This includes storage, metadata, and pending work needed for recovery, including accepted outgoing mail; it remains a target requiring validation. Brazil is selected for the first deployment/cost estimate, using AWS São Paulo (sa-east-1). The user has no AWS account yet. Consumers run in a mix of AWS and external locations, with the external traffic fraction unknown. Number of zones, Kubernetes implementation and cluster configuration, persistence backends, and recovery mechanisms remain undecided; other deployment modes remain in scope. No infrastructure has been provisioned.
- The resilient multi-site deployment profile must survive loss of an entire datacenter or cloud region without losing accepted email and resume accepting new mail and serving consumers within five minutes of the failure. Multiple independent sites can be provided for both self-hosted and cloud deployments, but the user explicitly defers the number of sites pending evaluation of topology and cost. See [site-failure durability](../adr/0010-survive-site-loss-without-accepted-mail-loss.md). This is an agreed recovery target, not a demonstrated capability; no two-site or three-site commitment has been made.

- After a site failure, resilient deployments may temporarily accept new email with weaker protection when insufficient independent storage sites remain, with operator alerts. These new receipts may not survive a further site loss before replication is restored; mail accepted under normal protection must still survive the initial covered failure. Durable storage on surviving infrastructure remains required. FRY automatically enters this reduced-protection mode and alerts operators once safe ownership has been established, without per-incident operator approval. Reduced-protection acceptance continues until replication recovers, without an elapsed-time cutoff, while surviving storage remains healthy and ownership remains safe; operator alerts continue throughout. Storage-health thresholds, capacity limits, mechanisms for safe ownership during partitions, and repair remain open.

Recovery guarantees vary explicitly by deployment profile. The no-loss/five-minute whole-site or region recovery target applies to resilient multi-site installations; the initial single-region AWS profile has the same no-loss/five-minute target for failure of one entire availability zone, rather than the whole region. Other single-site or regional profiles remain to be specified. On-premises describes hosting, not a resilience guarantee: its profile depends on actual site topology. Several sites within one cloud region do not provide an independent location outside that region.

## Dependent branches

- IMAP plus API/webhook access and human review → shared message visibility, flags/deletion semantics, routing interfaces, replay and acknowledgments, definition of inbox.
- Multiple addresses per inbox → alias management, recipient-based routing, preservation of original recipient information, one copy per inbox for overlapping recipients within a single SMTP submission while preserving relevant recipient-address metadata, and domain/address changes.
- All-matching metadata/attachment-type routing rules → one logical delivery per distinct matching application, required per-inbox fallback for unmatched messages, trusted versus claimed sender/recipient fields, attachment classification, explainable routing outcomes, configuration permissions, and rule versions during backlog/replay. Retry attempts remain distinct from additional rule matches. The fallback covers absence of a matching route; it has not been designated as an alternate destination when another application's delivery fails.
- Complete-email delivery with webhook notification plus authenticated download → application access to all attachments of a routed message, separately tracked notification receipt and delivery acknowledgment, download authorization/lifetime, and whole-message disposition when any attachment fails a security/format check. Partial attachment stripping or transformation has not been authorized as the delivery model.
- ZIP content inspection → member-type classification for routing, bounded expansion and work, member/nesting limits, recorded privileged overrides for password-protected ZIP quarantine, corrupt-archive handling, malware scanning of contents, and whether any other member-inspection failure blocks the whole email. An override of password protection permits delivery of the complete original email/ZIP without pretending its encrypted members were inspected or classified. Exact limits and inspection implementation require evaluation; no unbounded extraction is authorized by this scope decision.
- Dual ingress → MX changes, coexistence, provider connectors, polling/reconnection behavior, duplicate handling, acceptance and outage behavior. Optional source cleanup requires recoverable message bytes and required state across sites before deletion, plus checkpointing and retry-safe cleanup that avoids removing unrelated messages.
- Isolated organizations → exclusive incoming-domain ownership per organization within an installation, organization/domain/address relationships, domain verification and transfer, access control across every interface, quotas, storage/resource isolation and operator access.
- FRY's email/security and attachment-format checks → spam and malware policy, document recognition, malformed/unsupported content handling, quarantine and review. Business schema and semantic validation belong to receiving applications.
- Quarantine for uncertain spam, malware findings, and password-protected ZIPs → reviewer permissions, designated organization reviewers and central FRY operators for privileged overrides with mandatory recorded reason, preservation of original findings, review-interface visibility, release behavior, audit history, and review capacity. Unreleased mail expires after normal retention plus grace with alerts and a quarantine-expiry record.
- Outbound through an existing relay → authenticated submission, explicitly authorized sender domains/addresses per organization (including approved send-on-behalf-of identities), shared default versus organization-specific last-mile MTA selection, relay credentials and limits, FRY-owned durable outbound queue and retries, queue admission/capacity and retry limits, bounce handling, and distinction between relay handoff and final delivery.
- Existing storage bottleneck and workload evidence → sizing, retention, search, availability, migration and deployment choices.
- Acceptance and delivery contracts → durable storage, replay, duplicate handling and consumer acknowledgements.
- Independent application deliveries → per-application progress and failure isolation, routing subscriptions, acknowledgment mechanisms, retry/replay scope, and retention when some deliveries remain pending. API/webhook completion means confirmed safe acceptance; IMAP completion means safe availability in an application's independent mailbox view and does not establish that the application has fetched or processed the message.
- Independent IMAP mailbox views → application/view identity, per-view folders and flags, same-application concurrent sessions, UID continuity and takeover, view-level cleanup versus original retention, and metadata scale as the number of consuming applications grows.
- API/webhook retries with a stable delivery ID → consumer deduplication, identity persistence across failover, retry backoff and limits, terminal failure handling, and explicit replay semantics. A delivery ID identifies one application's handoff, not all deliveries of a message; deduplication of separate incoming emails or business documents is a different, unresolved concern.
- Buffered processing with fair service across organizations → recoverable pending work, checks before versus after acceptance, backlog limits and drain capacity, overload handling, scheduling/resource isolation, and latency tests for other organizations while one has a large backlog. Queue/broker choice and the fairness mechanism remain undecided.
- Large batches concentrated in one inbox with out-of-order processing/delivery allowed → within-inbox concurrency, preserved receipt timestamps, mailbox-view metadata growth, per-user/session coordination, header/date/flag search behavior, and takeover/recovery at retained-data scale. Distributing different inboxes across servers does not by itself establish capacity for this case; relaxing application delivery order does not remove protocol-state coordination requirements.
- Hold-and-retry on unavailable required filtering → durable pending-check state, retry scheduling/alerts, service recovery, and processing failure plus alerted expiry after retention and grace. Scanner unavailability must not be represented as a clean result or a positive malware finding.
- Retained originals independent of inbox deletion → separation of inbox state from original lifetime, recovery/replay authorization, retention scheduling and expiration, storage cost, and cleanup of related artifacts. Unacknowledged application deliveries, unavailable required filtering checks, and unreleased quarantine receive the same bounded retention-plus-grace policy with state-appropriate outcomes and alerts; this intentional expiry is distinct from losing accepted mail during infrastructure failure. Live messages in default shared mailboxes persist until client deletion, independently of recovery-copy expiry. Physical cleanup must respect all live references, even if a pending delivery expires. Permanent deletion does not start a new recovery window; old unreferenced messages may be cleaned up once all remaining retention/grace requirements end. Application to optional views or outbound originals still needs clarification.
- Self-hosted plus managed cloud support → portable persistence contracts, supported backend capabilities, operational responsibility, deployment/upgrade procedures, and failure-domain-specific durability. A shared API name alone does not establish equivalent backend behavior.
- No accepted-mail loss after whole-site failure → durable recoverable message bytes and required state outside the failed site before acknowledgment, cross-site coordination and latency, split-brain prevention, surviving-site capacity, and recovery/failback procedures. Topology and replication mechanisms have not been selected.

## Architecture interview closed

The product scope and preferred storage pattern are established. Physical reclamation is settled: within 24 hours of final-reference expiry by default, configurable, for active storage. The hot tier should be sized for six hours of incoming mail at the expected byte rate during S3-upload unavailability, configurable, while replicas and the database remain healthy. This is a capacity target, not an expiry period or acceptance timer; safe-capacity exhaustion defers SMTP receipt and pauses IMAP collection. Organization storage quotas are advisory: alert administrators and continue incoming SMTP receipt and IMAP collection while shared capacity is safe. Do not automatically delete mail or defer receipt solely for exceeding an organization quota. All three closing questions are answered; see the [consolidated architecture brief](architecture-brief.md). Physical safety limits and fair service remain applicable. Existing decisions about keeping live mail, receipt-based retention, bounded future-delivery expiry, and reduced-protection acceptance remain in force.

Unknown arrival rates, message-size distribution, and client behavior remain measurement/compatibility tasks, not questions to repeat when the user has already said they are unknown. Pack sizes, shard counts, append framing, retry implementation, and database indexes belong in proposed engineering designs and validation. Detailed administration/API and later optional-feature policies remain explicitly deferred specification work; closing the architecture interview will not mean these are silently settled or that production readiness is proven.

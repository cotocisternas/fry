# Preserve accepted email across datacenter or region loss

The resilient multi-site deployment profile of FRY must survive failure of an entire datacenter or cloud region without losing accepted email and resume accepting new mail and serving consumers within five minutes of the failure. Multiple independent sites can be provided for both deployment modes, but their number is explicitly deferred pending topology and cost evaluation. This is a target requiring validation; replication mechanisms, capacity, degraded-operation guarantees, and failover procedures remain open.

Recovery guarantees vary explicitly by deployment profile. The first AWS production profile uses one region across multiple availability zones and must survive loss of one entire availability zone without losing accepted email, restoring mail reception and mailbox access within five minutes of the failure. This target covers durable message bytes and the state needed to recover mailbox access and accepted pending work, including outgoing mail queued by FRY. It requires validation across compute, storage, metadata, networking, and surviving capacity; deploying multiple application replicas alone does not demonstrate it. Other single-site or regional profiles may have separately defined narrower guarantees. A single-region installation cannot promise recovery from complete loss of that region using only resources inside it. On-premises installations may use either single-site or resilient multi-site profiles, subject to their actual topology.

After a site fails, resilient deployments may temporarily accept new mail with weaker protection when too few independent storage sites remain, with alerts to operators. New receipts during this degraded period do not carry the normal protection against a further site loss until sufficient independent durable copies exist. This exception does not relax the requirement to preserve mail accepted under normal protection through the initial covered failure. Safe durable storage in the surviving infrastructure is still required. FRY enters this reduced-protection mode automatically and alerts operators once it has safely established which surviving servers may accept mail. Per-incident operator approval is not required. Reduced-protection acceptance continues until replication recovers, without an elapsed-time cutoff, while surviving storage remains healthy and ownership remains safe; operator alerts continue throughout. Storage-health thresholds, capacity limits, replication repair, and return to normal protection remain open; permission to reduce replication does not establish safe ownership during a network partition.

# Support direct reception and IMAP collection

FRY will receive email directly through SMTP and collect email from existing mailboxes through IMAP, supporting both controlled receiving domains and existing mailbox integrations. Source cleanup is configurable per connection and keeps copies by default; deletion may be enabled after durable collection across FRY's independent sites, with timing/mechanics and rollout order still open. Ongoing IMAP collection supports configurable source folders in the first milestone, rather than being limited to INBOX. This is separate from one-time mailbox history migration. Each selected source folder maps to a configurable destination folder in a FRY inbox; collected mail is not forced into INBOX. After collection, FRY keeps an independent copy: later source-side reads/flags, moves, and deletions do not modify or remove that copy. This does not change optional source cleanup after safe collection or the final synchronization required for one-time migration. Mapping configuration changes, destination-folder creation/rename/deletion behavior, source-folder selection/discovery, initial-copy metadata, and repeat-collection detection remain open. A moved message appearing under a new source-folder identity still needs an explicit duplicate-handling policy; independent copying does not establish cross-folder deduplication. Consumer access through FRY's IMAP server is a separate responsibility covered by [ADR 0003](0003-imap-and-api-consumers.md).

The first production rollout includes importing existing mailbox history and state for cutover, preserving folders, message dates, and read/unread flags. This migration requirement is distinct from ongoing IMAP collection; it does not by itself require continuous mirroring of every source-side move or deletion. Most history may be copied in the background, followed by a planned pause of affected clients while arrivals are queued or deferred and remaining changes are synchronized before switching. The planned pause must last no more than 30 minutes for affected mailboxes, excluding background copying. This is separate from the resilient deployment profile's five-minute failure-recovery target and requires cutover validation at representative scale. A coordinated reset of client checkpoints and resynchronization is acceptable if FRY assigns new IMAP identifiers; preservation of source identifiers is not required. This migration allowance does not relax identifier stability during normal FRY operation or failover. Resynchronization may cause old mail to be fetched again; duplicate-processing handling remains open. The 30-minute cutover limit requires mail reception and mailbox access to resume; clients may continue resynchronizing afterward. It is not a deadline for all clients to finish fetching history. Transfer mechanism, final synchronization mechanics, reset procedure, rollback, and verification remain open. Importing mail does not authorize deleting source history or decommissioning existing servers.

For direct SMTP reception, FRY rejects unconfigured recipient addresses by default. A domain may explicitly configure a catch-all inbox within its owning organization to receive mail addressed to otherwise unconfigured recipients. Catch-all handling is conventional address resolution in the first milestone, distinct from the deferred fallback application used by optional application routing. Explicitly configured recipients retain their configured destinations; the catch-all is not a copy of every domain message.

The first milestone supports distribution aliases: one configured incoming address may deliver to several destination inboxes within the same organization. Multiple addresses may also feed one inbox. This is conventional recipient-address resolution, independent of the deferred application-routing feature; it does not require application subscriptions or fallback applications. Each destination is a distinct inbox with its own mailbox state. For one SMTP submission, each distinct destination inbox receives one copy even when several recipient addresses or alias expansions resolve to it. Preserve the relevant recipient-address metadata for that inbox. This is scoped to a single submission and does not deduplicate separate SMTP submissions, messages sharing a Message-ID, or business documents. Address-resolution changes during pending receipt, metadata exposure, and alias configuration limits remain open. Cross-organization distribution aliases and external forwarding are not established by this decision.

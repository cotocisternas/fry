# Isolate organizations within FRY

Organization storage quotas are advisory: crossing a configured threshold triggers administrator alerts while new incoming SMTP mail and IMAP collection continue as long as shared capacity is safe. Quota exceedance alone does not defer receipt, pause collection, or automatically delete existing mail. Physical-capacity protection and fair service across organizations still apply. Exact accounting, alert thresholds/recipients, and treatment of outbound queues remain detailed specification work.

FRY will support multiple organizations with isolated domains, inboxes, and access permissions, even if the initial deployment serves only one business. Designated organization security reviewers may override malware findings or password-protected ZIP quarantine on their organization's mail, and central FRY operators may also exercise that privilege, with a recorded reason required in either case. This establishes an explicit review exception to organization isolation; shared versus dedicated storage/infrastructure and other privileged operations remain undecided. These filtering-related review privileges apply to the later optional-feature milestone.

The first milestone supports both central FRY operators and organization administrators through the management interfaces. Organization managers (also called organization administrators) are internal Customer Satisfaction and Professional Services staff from the user's company, rather than assumed customer-side administrators. Global FRY operators (also called central operators) are its Technology and Development teams. Organization managers administer domains, mailboxes, and credentials within their authorized organization scope; managers may administer only explicitly assigned organizations, potentially several per person. Membership in the internal teams does not by itself grant access to every customer organization. Only global operators and their configured OIDC mappings may grant or revoke organization-manager assignments; organization managers cannot delegate administrative access, including within organizations they already manage. Central operators provide administration across organizations in a deployment. In the first milestone, organization administrators may be granted explicit permission to open or download complete emails within their authorized organization scope, and central operators may be granted this permission across organizations in that deployment, for troubleshooting and recovery. Record content access in an audit log. Configuration authority alone does not grant this content-access permission. Impersonation of mailbox users, other privileged operations, audit fields/retention, and access controls for the audit log remain open.

Administrator sign-in in the first milestone supports both FRY-managed accounts and single sign-on. This is separate from application IMAP/SMTP credentials. Generic OpenID Connect (OIDC) support is required and must be available in the first milestone; initial operation will use FRY-managed accounts. No specific identity provider or SAML requirement is selected. One shared OIDC provider per independent FRY installation is sufficient initially; per-organization provider configuration is not required for the first milestone. On first OIDC login, FRY automatically provisions accounts and access from configured identity-provider group/claim mappings; an invitation or pre-created account is not required. Explicit configured group/claim mappings may also grant global/central-operator privileges. Exact mapping rules and provider group names, assignment workflow and reconciliation between local grants and OIDC mappings, treatment of unmatched identities, identity linking, and subsequent account/access lifecycle remain open.

Assigned organization managers and global operators may authorize outgoing sender domains and addresses, including send-on-behalf-of identities, within their respective organization scopes. This permission is distinct from granting manager assignments, which remains restricted to global operators and their configured OIDC mappings. Enabling a new outgoing sender domain or address requires verification of control or delegation, with a recorded override available only to global operators. Verification mechanisms and audit details remain open. This decision concerns outgoing sender authorization; incoming-domain onboarding remains to be specified.

Each incoming custom domain belongs to exactly one organization within a FRY installation. Different recipient addresses under that domain cannot be assigned to different organizations. An organization may still have multiple domains and addresses, including multiple addresses feeding one inbox and one address delivering to several inboxes within the same organization. This incoming-domain ownership constraint is distinct from approved outgoing send-on-behalf-of identities and does not impose global uniqueness across independent installations. Direct SMTP reception rejects unconfigured recipients by default; each domain may explicitly configure a catch-all inbox in its owning organization. Domain onboarding verification and ownership transfer remain open.
